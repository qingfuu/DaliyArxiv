<summary>完整摘要</summary>

The growing demand for on-orbit servicing and constellation management in low Earth orbit requires autonomous and reliable low-thrust rendezvous. Traditional numerical methods struggle with the long-horizon nature of multi-revolution maneuvers under perturbations, while standard deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods are limited by low simulation throughput and poor sample efficiency. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a massively parallel DRL guidance framework. A fully tensorized, GPU-resident orbital propagator is developed to integrate $J_2$-perturbed orbital dynamics and policy optimization within a zero-copy pipeline, achieving up to a 67-fold increase in simulation throughput over conventional CPU baselines. In addition, the rendezvous problem is formulated in modified equinoctial elements and trained with a progressive curriculum learning strategy to improve convergence and generalization across increasingly challenging rendezvous scenarios. Simulations show that the proposed framework can learn robust rendezvous trajectories for complex multi-revolution rendezvous over a broad range of orbital configurations. Additional evaluations under a high-fidelity model and eclipse-induced thrust interruptions further demonstrate the robustness of the learned policy under practical mission constraints. Moreover, the trajectories generated by the proposed framework can be used as effective initial guesses for optimization-based trajectory solvers, improving the convergence of high-precision trajectory optimization. The method offers a scalable approach for real-time low-thrust guidance and trajectory optimization.
